# M9.7 partial-range map preflight

The ARM64, RV64 and x86_64 page-table editors first count existing leaves in
the requested range. They reject a partly mapped range before changing a
leaf. This record prices that scan separately from the whole successful map
operation. The measurement instrument is the temporary
[`M9.7_map_probe.patch`](M9.7_map_probe.patch), applied to the tree at commit
"Enforce caller-held scheduler exclusion" in an isolated worktree. It is not
part of the kernel. The full per-run rows are in
[`M9.7_map_preflight.tsv`](M9.7_map_preflight.tsv), whose last column is the
SHA-256 of each run's console capture. The unedited captures are in
[`M9.7_map_preflight_captures/`](M9.7_map_preflight_captures/), with their
[`SHA-256 list`](M9.7_map_preflight_captures.sha256).

The probe takes the first successful map of at least three non-executable
pages, then remaps its first page and first three pages alternately 100 times
each. These are existing leaves; no table allocation is on the measured path.
All 3,000 remaps succeeded. ARM64 and RV64 use their bench images. The x86
bench image has `KICKOS_HAVE_ASPACE=0`, so x86 uses the dedicated X5 address
space image; each of its five captures reports all 176 existing arms `ok=1`.
Each of the ten ARM/RV bench captures ends in `bench: done` and `KOS-RC=0`.

Each cell below is the median of five per-run means in nanoseconds after
discarding the lowest and highest of the 100 samples in each run. The printed
integer means in the TSV are the input, so rounding can move these values by
about one nanosecond. The clock reads inside the scan and whole-map brackets
are included. `whole map` includes validation, the scan, leaf writes and any
invalidation; it is not an additive component beside the scan.

| QEMU TCG backend | Pages | Scan | Whole map |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ARM64 Cortex-A53 | 1 | 135 ns | 625 ns |
| ARM64 Cortex-A53 | 3 | 190 ns | 702 ns |
| RV64 virt | 1 | 211 ns | 767 ns |
| RV64 virt | 3 | 385 ns | 1,033 ns |
| x86_64 q35 X5 | 1 | 173 ns | 998 ns |
| x86_64 q35 X5 | 3 | 253 ns | 1,703 ns |

The clock each backend reads sets its resolution. Every ARM64 minimum and
maximum in the TSV is a multiple of 16 ns and every RV64 one a multiple of
100 ns, which are their timer ticks. The x86_64 ones share no divisor above
1 ns, its clock being the TSC scaled to nanoseconds. The 211 ns one-page RV64
scan is about two ticks.

The one-page ARM64 run has a cold outlier in each capture, reaching 5.7 to
8.5 microseconds for the scan; the raw means therefore misleadingly put one
page above three. The trimmed result and raw min/mean/max are both recorded,
without turning that emulator effect into a kernel scaling claim.

This bounds only these QEMU images and these already-mapped ranges. QEMU's
translation, cache, timer and invalidation costs are not silicon costs. The
probe does not measure a first map that allocates tables, a failed partial
range, a resident multi-core shootdown, or a large range. It shows the scan
is measurable and grows with page count in these small cases; it does not
justify changing the reject-before-edit rule. A KVM run would measure the
host processor plus virtualization, still not a bare-metal KickOS map cost.
