# M10.1 exit measurement

The `bench` image of the four one-core QEMU bench presets and of the three M8.12 reference
boards, on two trees built in the same session with the same toolchains: the
[M10.1.2 baseline](M10.1.2_baseline.md)'s tree, "Design the M10.1 ABI changes and take their
baseline", and the tree M10.1 closes on, "Size the address-space range default to the selftest
again". The toolchains are CI's pins, Arm and AArch64 15.2.rel1 and RISCstar 16.1-r1, with the
host's g++ 16.2 on x86_64 and each target's newlib built by `conan/newlib`, whose recipe the two
trees share. Every figure is the bench's own report in nanoseconds, and the
[raw captures](M10.1_exit_captures) are the whole serial output of each run;
[`M10.1_exit_captures.sha256`](M10.1_exit_captures.sha256) digests them. The board captures are
archived as the baseline's were, CRLF normalised to LF and the one byte above 0x7F each F411 and
ESP32-C6 capture held written as `?`.

A cell reads baseline / M10.1 (change), each side the median of ten boots on QEMU and of three
captures on a board. Half-nanosecond QEMU medians are shown without the fractional part.

**The boards are the figures to read.** The baseline tree repeated M10.1.2's recorded silicon
figures to the nanosecond, and every board cell repeated across its three captures. On silicon
M10.1 costs a spawn 2 to 4 percent, the task-creation authority, the creator's reports and the
window list being checked on every spawn, and a call/reply 2 to 3 percent; a switch got
slightly cheaper. The XMC's cycle counter read dead in some captures, the condition M8.12
recorded, so its bench refused the cycle rows there; the rows above are wall-clock and stand.

**A QEMU cell is compared only within this session, and the emulator ran pinned.** This host has
cores of two speeds, 5.2 and 3.3 GHz. Unpinned, a boot landed on either and the rows split into
two clusters, so a median said which cluster most boots had landed in: the x86 thread spawn read
+47 percent in an unpinned run and +17 percent pinned. Every QEMU capture here ran on two of the
fast cores, alternating baseline and M10.1 boot for boot, both x86 images on `qemu64` with the
x86-64-v3 features. rv32 still splits in two with the emulator pinned, in both trees alike, so its
medians compare but its spread, about half the median, is not the kernel's.

**x86's rows split across three parts.** A second session of ten boots of four trees, alternated,

The authority, reports and window parts cost a spawn 3 to 5 percent, as they do elsewhere. The
port grant costs a switch 2 to 3 percent, its port-set compare, and a spawn 2 to 4. Moving the
build to x86-64-v3 coincides with another 9 percent on thread spawn and 5 percent on same-task
switch **under TCG only**. Those figures include QEMU's handling of the changed instruction stream
and say nothing about x86 hardware. The same-task call/reply swings 19 percent up at
M10.1.8 and 5 down after it, the noise this row has shown between sessions before
(`M10.1.1_bench.md`); no part is charged with it.

**Static figures**, the baseline's table on both trees:

| | armv6m (micro:bit) | armv7m (Blue Pill) | rv32 | armv8a, rv64 | x86_64 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `sizeof(Thread)` | 280 / 280 | 272 / 272 | 280 / 280 | 496 / 480 | 480 / 488 |
| `sizeof(Task)` | 12 / 20 | 12 / 20 | 12 / 20 | 16 / 24 | 16 / 24 |
| `sizeof(Endpoint)` | 24 / 24 | 24 / 24 | 24 / 24 | 48 / 48 | 48 / 48 |

A task gains 8 bytes, the creator's watch M10.1.5 added. The thread changed in three parts, the
wider authority word, the window list and, on x86, the port set: 8 bytes more on x86 and 16
fewer on armv8a and rv64, net.
`hello` on the micro:bit, against the nano newlib the board ships, goes from 37,100 bytes of
text, 132 of data and 11,000 of bss to 38,964, 132 and 11,096; on the Blue Pill, against the
full one, from 33,520, 376 and 7,328 to 35,392, 376 and 7,408. The baseline recorded the Blue
Pill at 568 bytes of data and 7,904 of bss because its tree was built with Arm 15.3.rel1, whose
newlib 4.6.0 carries a larger `struct _reent`; built with CI's 15.2.rel1, as both trees are
here, the baseline tree gives the figures above.
