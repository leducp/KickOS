# M8.7 -- the rebaseline campaign

The PRE-OPTIMISATION baseline. M8.8 through M8.11 are attributed against it; **M8.12 is the
M8-exit measurement and M9 is judged against that one, never against this.**

Taken with the instrument complete: per-core accumulators, cycle sources on arm64/rv64/x86_64,
the named distribution family, the lock hold and wait, the doorbell round, the IRQ-to-userspace
span with its locality split, per-core switch stamps, and 64-bit spans with saturation counting.

**THE EVIDENCE IS THIS FILE.** The silicon captures are below, whole, one section each, and every
published silicon row is read off them. Three things were done to the bytes and nothing else: the
carriage returns are stripped; the single non-ASCII framing byte a console cable emits as the
reader arms, ahead of the first banner, is dropped; and on the Espressif part the boot-ROM
preamble ahead of the last banner box is dropped. The `sha256` of each ORIGINAL is in the manifest
at the end, so anyone holding one can reproduce the text here. **The thirty emulator runs are NOT
here**: they cost no bench time and a reader with this tree and `qemu` regenerates them, which is
the test `docs/README.md` states for what this directory is for. Their hashes are in the manifest
so a copy can be identified, which is an identity check and not evidence.

**BOTH HALVES ARE ON THE TREE THEY SHIP WITH.** Every capture embedded below was taken on
this tree, one commit before this file, and every silicon row in the tables is read off those six
logs. The previous pass's captures were taken before the end-to-end sweep's denominator existed,
so their reports carry no `e2e-passes` line and the baseline they gave was a mixed one; they are
gone from this file. The thirty emulator runs were stored before the console fix and the capture
tooling that followed it and carried no denominator either, so they were taken again on this same
tree; what that moved is in "What moved" below. The banners name this file's PARENT commit, since
a record cannot be captured on the commit that contains it; nothing but this file, `TODO.md` and
`STATE.md` separates the two.

**So the suspicion that drove the milestone is largely disproved, and that is the result worth
keeping.** Four landed changes were expected to have moved this table and did not move it on
this board -- which is consistent rather than surprising once stated: `esp32c6-wroom` is a

**What this does not restore.** Section 3.0.4's `f = 0.457` and 1.37x remain superseded. They
were derived by removing 886 cycles of `MPU_APPLY` from a 3651-cycle locked round trip; that
term is now 89 a switch, 178 a round trip, so the multiplier does not survive and no
replacement is derived from composites here -- see the next section for why, and the lock
measurement for what replaces it.

## The locked fraction is now MEASURED, not derived

The outermost lock's hold time is a first-class distribution. On `f411disco`, over the
ping-pong burst, `lock-hold` reports p50 768 cycles at n = 80022 against 40000 switches -- two
outermost lock spans per switch, plus the 22 the sweep's own setup takes -- against a measured
6700 cycles per round. That
is **about 46 percent of that workload spent inside the outermost lock**, with no composite
arithmetic and no correction constant. The 43-to-53 percent bracket section 3.0.4 derived for
the call/reply workload is the figure this supersedes in kind.

## Per board

Emulator figures are five runs each; silicon is two a board, for the determinism reason below.
The switch `p50` was the same figure in all five runs on four of six emulator boards and took
two adjacent values on the other two, at a host load of five to seven throughout.

**THE ARMV7M CAPTURES USED TO STOP ONE LINE SHORT, AND THE WINDOW WAS NOT THE REASON.** Both
armv7m boards ended without `bench: done`, which the app writes last, and this record read that
as the capture window closing on the sentinel. It was not. Re-taken with a 300 s window against
about 45 s of work, the boards still printed nothing after the last report row, on every run.
The app writes its own lines through the kernel console, which answers a SHORT COUNT while its
transmit ring is still draining, and that answer was dropped unread: a line issued immediately
behind a kernel print burst was lost whole. The sentinel is the only line the bench writes in
that position, which is why it alone went missing and why `esp32c6-wroom` kept its own. Fixed in
this tree: every capture below carries the sentinel, and no capture on this bench is refused for
anything.

A FOURTH SHORT-RETURN SITE WAS CONSIDERED AND DECLINED: `kernel/init/console.cc:354` guards
`cap_console_deliver` with `== 0`, where a short return is possible, and force-writing the line
to the chip is not a fallback there -- console ownership is exclusive, so while the console is
`USER_OWNED` the device belongs to the driver and a chip write goes somewhere that is not the
console. Truncation is truncation: the answers are to accept it or to retry the remainder, never
to duplicate the line to a different sink. Recorded so the next audit pass does not re-raise it.

## What these figures may NOT be used for

- **No emulator figure is a cycle count of any real part.** A QEMU tick is instruction-path
  length under TCG. The three first figures are comparable to each other across M8.7 to M8.12
  on the same emulator, which is what an attribution baseline needs, and to nothing else.
- **The four-core ratios are not kernel costs.** arm64 costs 2.4x a one-core switch and rv64
  5.9x, and the two are not comparable to each other either: rv64's `rdcycle` reads the HOST
  TSC while arm64's `PMCCNTR_EL0` reads QEMU virtual time, so host time inside a bracket is
  counted on one and largely not on the other. Nothing on this bench can separate the kernel's
  share from the emulator's, there being no multi-core silicon here.
- **Composites on arm64, rv64, x86_64, the LX6 and the sim are not costs.** The switch swaps
  inline there, so a composite closes when the thread is next resumed. Above one core it is not
  even representable: the bracket opens on one core and closes on another and the counter is
  per core, which the instrument now counts as `SAT` rather than wrapping. **A row carrying
  `SAT` is not published as a cost here, and every such row is a switch-enclosing composite --
  every leaf on the four-core boards is clean.**
- **`max` off an emulator is a statement about the box.** `p50` held to one bucket across every
  window at host load five to seven; `max` moved by up to two orders of magnitude in the same
  runs -- `qemu-riscv-bench` reported a switch max of 16020 cycles in one run and 982680 in
  another, with `p50` at 1536 in all five.
- **`p50` and `p99` are bucket LOW edges** at an eighth of an octave, so both are floors and two
  runs compare only to that resolution.
- **A PERCENTILE IS ONLY A PERCENTILE WHERE THE SAMPLE COUNT SUPPORTS ITS RANK, and this file
  prints one label for two kinds of row.** The WORKLOAD-FED rows are fed by the ping-pong burst
  and carry tens of thousands of samples: `switch` at n = 40002 puts 400 samples above the 99th
  rank and `lock-hold` at n = 80022 puts 800, so their `p99` means what it says. The SWEPT rows
  are a fixed short sweep and do not: `e2e-local` at n = 50 has its 99th rank AT rank 50, so its
  `p99` IS the maximum wearing a percentile's label; `irq` and every `wcase-irq` at n = 100, and
  `e2e-cross` at n = 103 to 150, put about ONE sample above the rank. **No swept row's `p99` may be
  differenced against an M8.12 row.** The rule this file already states for `max` off an emulator
  -- that it is a statement about the box -- extends to every swept `p99`, on silicon too, and
  the `e2e-local` move recorded below is the direct evidence for it.
- **Nothing about the instrument was changed to enforce that**, deliberately: every row already
  prints its own `n`, so the rank is checkable per row by anyone reading a capture, and moving
  the instrument mid-baseline would void the captures this file is made of. Refusing to print a
  `p99` below some `n`, or labelling a swept row `max` outright, is an M8.12-era question.
- **The IRQ block is not in CI and no figure here was taken there.** CI is a regression test and
  not a performance one, and every arm of that gate reads a timing distribution off a runner
  whose load it does not control. The block is witnessed by hand at each rebaseline, which is
  this file. The instrument's other gates -- the counter, the lock probe, the saturation column,
  the phase-table reconciliation and the authority arms -- are regression tests and do run in CI,
  at one kernel core and at four, and on `qemu-x86_64-bench` through the firmware job. The
  four-core step is the only place the per-core and doorbell gates run at all.
- **The end-to-end span's last leg is a granted arena block, not a device register.** No board
  the bench runs under an emulator can grant a user thread a DEV window.
- **THE FOUR-CORE IRQ ROWS OF THE FIRST PASS ARE RETRACTED.** `qemu-arm64-benchsmp` published
  `irq: 983040` and two masked rows beside it, `qemu-riscv64-benchsmp` five and six figures, and
  this record read them as the raiser spinning until a peer took the line. **That reading was
  wrong.** The peer stamps its OWN cycle counter and the counters share no zero, so the figure
  was the offset between two clocks: a wait would have grown with the masked body and it did
  not. The probe now names the core the sweep ran on, the core the handler stamped on, and the
  raise-to-observation window, and it refuses a sample failing either test. Both boards print
  `n=0` on every entry and masked row, nearly always beside `foreign=100` -- `hcore=0` against
  `on=3` on arm64, where the GIC routes the free SPI to one core, and `hcore=mixed` on rv64,
  where `sip.SSIP` is double-booked with the kernel doorbell and whichever hart next takes one
  swallows the bit. A handful of rv64 rows read `hcore=none foreign=0 win=0` instead: there no
  handler stamped at all, so there was nothing to call foreign.
  **The four-core IRQ figure of this campaign is the end-to-end span and nothing else**, that
  span reading one clock for the whole image.
- **The IRQ-entry row is void wherever the syscall that raised the line runs masked.** That is
  `armv8a` at every core count, entering EL1 with `PSTATE.I` set, and `rv64imac` at one hart.
  An empty entry row beside closed end-to-end spans is that backend; an empty entry row beside
  no closed span is a board that cannot deliver an injected line at all, and the gate
  discriminates them.

## The XMC4800's cycle counter is intermittent, and every reading is on record

Its DWT sits in the core debug power domain, and the same physical board has now answered four
ways across five sessions. The first campaign read `0/0/0` over 40002 samples in both captures
and the capture chain REFUSED the cycle rows. An earlier session read `78/78/294`. A session
between the two read a live counter at 83 cycles a switch and, two hours later on the next tree,
a constant again. **The captures embedded below read LIVE in both cycles**, 83 cycles a switch
once more, over 21 sampled rows with none of them a constant. Nothing in the rig or the capture
recipe changed across any of those.

So the counter is intermittent rather than dead, and no one reading is the board's character:
a capture carrying no cycle row says nothing about the part, and one carrying a cycle row cannot
be assumed reproducible. The `xmc4800-relax` cycle cells above are what these two captures read
and are marked as such; the wall-clock cells are the ones a later milestone may difference
against.

The board declares `KICKOS_CHIP_CYCCNT_GLITCHES`, so its headline is min, avg and max rather than
a percentile: a glitched read can only inflate a delta, never push one below the true minimum.
Its cells are therefore not comparable with the percentile boards' cell for cell even when the
counter answers. Its wall-clock figures come from CCU40, a real hardware counter, and stand
either way. **`f411disco` is why armv7m has a distribution at all.**

## What moved against the previous captures

The captures this file used to embed were taken before the end-to-end sweep published its own
denominator, so they were a mixed baseline: their reports carry no `e2e-passes` line and their
banners name a tree the record no longer ships. These six were taken again on the tree this file
ships with. What follows is the comparison between the two sets, and it is the only reading here
of what eight commits of instrument work cost a figure.

**EVERY PERCENTILE HELD ON BOTH PERCENTILE BOARDS, AND ONE DID NOT.** `f411disco` reports the
same `switch` 80/80, the same `lock-hold` 768/1152, the same `irq` 36/36 and the same `e2e-local`
28672/30720; `esp32c6-wroom` the same 144/144, 704/1024, 192/192, and the same four `wcase-irq`
p50s and p99s as before. Every `max` beside them moved, in both directions and by single-sample
amounts, which is what a maximum over one run does.

**`esp32c6-wroom`'s `e2e-local` p99 came back to 16384 ns from 45056, with `max` at 16657 against
48988.** That is the same row, and the same one sample, the previous pass named: `e2e-local`
carries n = 50, so the nearest rank for a 99th percentile is rank 50 and this cell IS the
maximum. It moved down this time as it moved up last time, and the `p50` is 16384 in both.
**A reader differencing this cell against anything is differencing one sample.**

**THE IPC CALL LEAVES MOVED BY ONE TO FOUR CYCLES AND THE SWITCH LEAVES DID NOT.** On
`esp32c6-wroom`, `CALL_RESOLVE` 136 to 134, `CALL_PEEK` and `CALL_PROBE` 16 to 17, `CALL_POP` 50
to 49, `CALL_COPY` 89 to 90, `CALL_MINT_CAP` 288 to 284, `CALL_MINT_INFO` 88 to 87 and
`CALL_PARK` 175 to 174, while `WAKE_UNPARK`, `PICK_NEXT`, `SWITCH_BOOK`, `KTIME_REARM`,
`ARCH_SWITCH`, `MPU_APPLY` and `MPU_COMMIT` are identical to the cycle. The headline table at the
top of this file carries the new column. The leaves are tens of cycles, so a one-cycle move is
the last digit of a figure whose bucket resolution was never finer than that.

**THE XMC's COUNTER ANSWERED, AND ITS ROW IS NO LONGER A REFUSAL.** Both captures read a live
DWT where the previous pass read a constant in both and was refused for it. That is the board's
intermittency and not a change in the tree; the section above it carries every reading this bench
has taken.

**THE WALL CLOCK IS SLOWER BY A FRACTION OF A PERCENT ON EVERY BOARD.** The ping-pong throughput
reads 25088 against 25133 on `f411disco`, 47995 against 48491 on `esp32c6-wroom` and 42813
against 42788 on `xmc4800-relax`; the 8 B round trip moves the same way. It is a hardware timer
over a re-flashed image and the moves are inside the spread the first pass recorded.

**THE EMULATOR HALF IS RE-TAKEN ON THIS TREE, AND FIVE OF ITS TWELVE CYCLE CELLS DID NOT MOVE
AT ALL.** `qemu-riscv`'s switch 1536, `qemu-riscv64-benchsmp`'s switch 1408 and `lock-hold`
65536, and `qemu-x86_64`'s switch 60 and `lock-hold` 2304 read exactly what they read before.
The other seven moved by one to three buckets: switch `p50` 208 to 176 at one arm64 core,
448 to 416 at four, 208 to 240 at one rv64 hart; `lock-hold` 5632 to 6144, 3072 to 3840,
28672 to 26624 and 7168 to 7680. Every moved cell but one lies inside the run-to-run spread the
previous pass recorded for it; the exception is four-core arm64's `lock-hold`, whose new spread
ends where the old one began. Four-core only, `lock-wait` reads 15360 to 14336 on arm64 and
40960 unchanged on rv64, and the `doorbell` round 14336 to 13312 and 24576 to 30720. The wall
clock moved in both directions by up to nine percent, which is a loaded box and not a kernel.

**THE END-TO-END CELLS ARE THE LOOSEST THING IN THE TABLE AND THE ONE BIG NUMBER IS ONE RUN.**
arm64's `e2e-cross` p50 reads 131072 ns against 212992, and 212992 is exactly what the largest of
this pass's own five runs reported; the other four read 131072 or 147456. `e2e-local` holds at
81920 over runs spanning 65536 to 131072, and rv64's pair moves 106496 to 98304 and 180224 to
163840. These are swept rows of fifty to a hundred and fifty samples on a box under load, so the
ratio they support is the one stated above and no cell of them is worth differencing alone.

**REPEAT CYCLES WERE DROPPED BECAUSE DETERMINISM WAS MEASURED.** Within one image the two logs
are byte-identical apart from the build stamp: every cycle figure, every nanosecond and every
sample count repeats exactly across separate flashes. A third capture of one image therefore
binds nothing, so this pass takes two a board and states the determinism rather than spending the
cycles to re-measure it. Across a DIFFERENT image only `max` and the throughput line's last
digits move, both being single-sample extremes.
