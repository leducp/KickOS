# M9.5 pinned x86 owner-local IPC experiment (2026-09-26)

This records the withdrawn writer-barrier experiment from commit `2520a67d`
against the [unchanged BKL baseline](M9.5_x86_ipc_pinned.md), as described in
[the M9.5 design](../design-m9.5-local-ipc.md). The current kernel contains
only the BKL path. Each QEMU vCPU was pinned to a distinct physical host core
before OVMF started. Host CPUs 0-3 have a 5.16 GHz maximum; 4-11 have a
3.29 GHz maximum. The test ran 50,000 calls with a checked marker byte per
pair at 8 and 256 bytes, three repetitions per width. All pairs completed,
and their active intervals overlapped. The
[verified per-run TSV](M9.5_x86_ipc_local.tsv) and ignored raw captures under
`build/m95/local{2,4,8,12}/phase-r{1,2,3}/` retain the rates, windows and pin
maps.

| Physical host CPUs | Guest cores and pairs | Payload | Baseline median/s | Local median/s | Gain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0-1 | 2 | 8 B | 643,656 | 1,013,825 | 1.58x |
| 0-1 | 2 | 256 B | 630,993 | 997,279 | 1.58x |
| 0-3 | 4 | 8 B | 642,647 | 1,948,172 | 3.03x |
| 0-3 | 4 | 256 B | 638,494 | 1,929,519 | 3.02x |
| 4-11 | 8 | 8 B | 368,889 | 2,360,121 | 6.40x |
| 4-11 | 8 | 256 B | 368,861 | 2,353,038 | 6.38x |
| 0-11 | 12 | 8 B | 286,558 | 2,850,847 | 9.95x |
| 0-11 | 12 | 256 B | 282,594 | 2,860,109 | 10.12x |

The local sweep's 8-byte ranges were 1,012,899-1,019,561 at two cores,
1,944,456-1,948,337 at four, 2,358,872-2,362,534 at eight, and
2,830,667-2,854,960 at twelve. These are aggregate rates, not per-core
latencies. They show substantial scaling over the BKL control, but less than
linear scaling from the CPU-0 control to all twelve physical cores.

Two matched controls used the same four-core guest configuration:

| Workload | BKL median/s | Local-option median/s | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One 8-byte IPC pair pinned to core 0 | 560,866 | 545,857 | -2.68% |
| One 256-byte IPC pair pinned to core 0 | 553,597 | 537,559 | -2.90% |
| Four workers yielding under the global lock | 8,759,786 | 8,372,182 | -4.42% |

The single-pair images used `KICKOS_M95_BENCH_PAIRS=1` and alternated control
and candidate order across three repetitions. The yield images each ran two
million yields per core, also in alternating order, with every vCPU pinned to
host CPUs 0-3. Their captures are under `build/m95/one4base/phase-r*/`,
`build/m95/one4local/activated-r*/`, `build/m95/yield-base-late-r*/`, and
`build/m95/yield-activated-fast-r*/`. The latter is a global-only workload:
the barrier never activates, but the enabled check still adds cost. An earlier
eager writer barrier lost 15.03% on this control; one-time activation reduced
that loss to 4.42%.

The candidate passed the x86 SMP KVM selftest at four and twelve cores. Its
experimental `m95_local_park_migrates` arm moved a blocked caller from core 0
to core 1 before the reply; the caller resumed on core 1 and its saved local
bracket reacquired global exclusion. The x86 trap-depth gate and the static
style and atomic gates also passed. These tests covered the exercised transactions; they do
not establish a performance result for notification-heavy, timed, or unpinned
IPC, which takes the global path.

Recheck the retained captures with:

```sh
python3 tools/bench/m95_local_report.py build/m95 > docs/archive/M9.5_x86_ipc_local.tsv
```

The reporter checks the recorded one-to-one vCPU pin map, each pair's call
count, overlap, completion and exit status before printing a row. This is a
KVM result on one host and does not replace the M9.4 LX6 silicon gate. The
experiment was removed after the [mixed-workload decision](M9.5_x86_ipc_mixed.md).
