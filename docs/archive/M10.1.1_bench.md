# M10.1.1 x86_64 address spaces: the bench before and after

The `qemu-x86_64-bench` preset's `bench.efi`, one core. "Before" is the tree at "Measure
cross-task switches and spawns in the bench", the last commit before q35 gained address spaces
(`docs/design-m10-kernel-share.md` section 1); "after" is the tree at "Record the q35
address-space cost and what it leaves". Every figure is the bench's own report, in emulated
nanoseconds, and the [raw captures](M10.1.1_bench_captures) are the whole serial output of each
run. QEMU's TCG counts work rather than cycles, and times it against the host clock, which is how
the figures are read.

**The comparison is the paired one: twenty boots in one session, before and after alternating**
(`paired-before-*.log`, `paired-after-*.log`).

| Row, median ns | before | after | ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| same-task ping-pong, per switch | 2,313 | 1,929 | 0.8 |
| same-task call/reply, 16 B | 7,991 | 7,829 | 1.0 |
| cross-task ping-pong, per switch | 2,057 | 7,525 | 3.7 |
| cross-task call/reply, 16 B | 6,671 | 24,267 | 3.6 |
| thread spawn/exit | 26,767 | 38,471 | 1.4 |
| task spawn/exit | 29,058 | 85,188 | 2.9 |

Nothing that stays in one space got dearer. A switch between spaces costs about 3.6 times what it
did, which is the price section 1.7 names: PCIDs are off and the firmware's leaves are not global,
so every CR3 write drops every translation, the kernel half's included, and under TCG that is a
flush of the emulator's own TLB. A task spawn/exit costs 2.9 times, building and tearing down a
space. A thread spawn/exit costs 1.4 times, which matches what `HAS_ASPACE` adds to every spawn, a
stack taken from the frame pool and mapped into the task's space (`kernel/mem/ustack.cc`); that
phase is not timed on its own. The global kernel half and PCIDs are the levers for the
cross-space rows.

**Two sessions do not compare.** The first capture sets, ten runs before (`before-*.log`) and ten
after (`after-*.log`) taken hours apart, put the same-task call/reply at 6,666 and 11,585 and read
as a 74 percent rise. The paired run shows none, and on unchanged code the same row read 11,585 in
one session and 9,741 in the next, while armv8a's read 28,444 and 19,244 (`arm64-*.log` against
M10.1.2's baseline). So a before and an after are taken in one session, interleaved, or they are
not compared.
