# M4.7.9 teardown latency: what a surviving thread actually loses when a peer dies

**Captured 2026-08-07 against `23b9abb`** (M4.7.9 tip), xmc4800-relax and frdmk64f, both MPU-enforcing,
`bench` variant, `kickos_services_none`. 296 deaths per board. Zero missed arms, zero pool refusals,
zero asserts, zero panics. Its sibling capture for this milestone measures SPACE rather than time:
`M4.7.9_footprint_meas.md`, what a minimal posture earns and what diagnostics cost.

The question this answers is the one the milestone exists for: a motor thread and a com thread; the
com thread crashes; the motor thread must keep meeting its cycle. Supervision is liveness-by-activity
("no new targets for 10 cycles, go safe"), so what matters is not whether the survivor eventually
runs but whether it MISSES A CYCLE.

## Instrument, and its honest limits

`ktime_now()` (CCU40 on the XMC, chained PIT on the K64F). NOT DWT, which is documented unreliable on
both parts and free-runs at 0 on the K64F.

| | xmc4800-relax (144 MHz) | frdmk64f (120 MHz) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| per-stamp cost, amortised over 1000 masked reads | 820 ns | 533 ns |
| smallest resolvable step between two reads | 902 ns | 633 ns |
| `kos_clock_now()` syscall, the userspace peer's granularity | 3371 ns | 4847 ns |

**Run-to-run spread was below resolution**: the frozen window came back as 55264 ns on all 8 XMC
deaths and 78167 ns on all 8 K64F deaths, identical to the nanosecond. No difference below 1 us is
reported anywhere in this capture.

One instrument self-model failure, disclosed rather than smoothed over: predicted probe cost is
`2 * chunks * per-stamp`, and the measured pass-B minus pass-A difference agrees to 8% on the XMC at
every chunk width but disagrees by 60 to 80% on the K64F (in-situ ~950 ns/stamp against the 533 ns the
amortised loop reports, because the loop keeps the AIPS/PIT path warm and an isolated read does not).
**Quote the XMC for per-chunk figures.** K64F totals, taken with stamping off, are unaffected.

## The subject was a realistic dying thread

All five `cap_teardown` arms exercised on every death, proven by the peers' own return codes in every
row: `waiter=-130` (`EOWNERDEAD`, mutex force-unlock), `snd1=-32 snd2=-32` (`EPIPE`, endpoint
`recv_holders` reaching 0 with two parked senders), `cli=-32` (`EPIPE`, outstanding reply capability),
plus a claimed IRQ line and a run of owned semaphores. Swept-slot count tracks exactly: 4 / 8 / 12 / 14
occupied of a 15-slot child table. The table is 14/15 rather than 15/15 only because slot 0 (stdout) is
empty under `kickos_services_none`.

## There are TWO windows, not one

The `THREAD FAULT` dump runs BEFORE `dying` is set, so it is preemptible. Only what follows is frozen.
nsem=10 (14 slots), chunk=4, FIFO, stamping off:

| | xmc4800-relax | frdmk64f |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fault to stub (exception return plus redirect) | 2.3 us | 2.6 us |
| `THREAD FAULT` dump, PREEMPTIBLE | 105.3 us | 132.1 us |
| `cap_teardown` sweep | 50.6 us | 72.7 us |
| **FROZEN: nothing of any priority runs** | **55.3 us** | **78.2 us** |
| total, fault to off the ready set | 162.9 us | 212.9 us |

- A peer ABOVE the dying thread loses the FROZEN window: **55 us / 78 us**.
- A peer at or BELOW it loses the total: **163 us / 213 us**.

The prologue's preemptibility is witnessed rather than asserted: the whole-window switch delta is 1
(XMC) / 2 (K64F) while the sweep-only delta is 0, and the K64F total is bimodal (212.9 or 256.9 us) by
exactly the 44.1 us a peer spent running inside the dump.

## Scaling

Sweep against occupied capability slots (chunk=4, FIFO): 4 slots 27.8/33.4 us, 8 slots 38.1/53.8 us,
12 slots 46.6/66.7 us, 14 slots 50.6/72.7 us. That is **2.3 us/slot (XMC)** and **3.2 us/slot (K64F)**.
Extrapolated to a 64-slot ceiling: ~150 us / ~210 us of sweep. That extrapolation is a 4-point line, not
a measurement: `KICKOS_CAP_TABLE_SUPPLY` is 16 on both boards and no wider table was reachable.

Against `KCAP_TEARDOWN_CHUNK` (nsem=10), the per-chunk masked window scales with width as designed
(XMC: 5.0 us at W=1 to 40.5 us at W=16) but **the total sweep does not** (49.8 to 54.2 us across a 16x
range, 9 to 13%). So today that knob bounds ISR latency ONLY. It buys a peer thread nothing, because no
thread can preempt between chunks anyway.

## The priority deflate was measured and REJECTED

| | xmc frozen | k64f frozen | sweep preempted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| deflate off | 55.3 us | 78.2 us | 0 / 8 |
| deflate on | 57.1 us | 81.3 us | 0 / 8 |

It buys nothing and costs ~1.9 us (XMC) / ~3.1 us (K64F). With the dying thread demoted to priority 1,
a **priority-20** peer woken mid-sweep still waited 34.6 us and the sweep was still never preempted.

The reason is structural and is the real finding: `sched::wake()` carries
`if (kernel().current->dying) { return; }`, a BLANKET check, so a deflate has no reschedule to act on.
**A priority deflate is inert while that guard stands.**

## The `tick_rr` contradiction, settled empirically

`sched::tick_rr()` calls `reschedule()` with no `dying` check, so an RR dying thread IS switched out
mid-teardown, which is precisely what `wake()`'s comment says is unsafe. It needs two conditions at
once: a sweep longer than the 20 us `KICKOS_TIMER_MIN_DELTA_NS` floor, and an equal-or-higher-priority
READY peer, which the suppressed wakes themselves supply.

Observed at RR q=20 us: the sweep was preempted on 8/8 deaths on both boards. With an equal-priority
peer and chunk width 1: 48/48 deaths, up to 8 (XMC) / 13 (K64F) preemptions inside one sweep,
stretching it to 448 us / 891 us of wall clock while the higher-priority peer was served.

**Nothing broke** across 592 silicon plus 488 sim deaths, including a 200-iteration soak whose woken
peers close capabilities and create and lock fresh objects the instant they wake: no assert, no panic,
all five arms total on every death, and the sem/mutex/endpoint pools still allocating on the last
iteration.

This corroborates a structural argument already in the tree: `g_cap.teardown_depth` is a COUNT, not a
flag, and its own comment states that an RR slice expiry can switch a dying thread out and let a second
thread finish its sweep first. **The design already accommodates the mid-sweep switch, so the blanket
guard looks like starvation rather than safety.**

## Fault death against `kos_exit`

Frozen window: `kos_exit` 53.2 / 76.6 us, fault 55.3 / 78.2 us. **The same to within 2 us.** The fault
path's entire extra cost is the diagnostic print, which is preemptible. So the new path changes nothing
for a higher-priority survivor and triples the window for an equal-or-lower one.

The print is also the one UNBOUNDED term. Under console pressure (48 back-to-back deaths) it grew from
105 us to 290 us (XMC) / 876 us (K64F), because `console_tx_write`'s overflow branch drains the
512-byte ring synchronously under `IrqLock` at ~87 us/byte. The measured runs never crossed fully into
it, so that cost is computed rather than observed, but the cliff is real and it dwarfs `cap_teardown`.

## The ruling

**No deflate. Repair `sched::wake()`'s guard instead, and not in this milestone.**

Budget assumed, and stated so it can be argued with: a 10 kHz FOC current loop (100 us period) tripping
safety at 10 silent cycles (1 ms).

1. The number is already inside that budget: 55 us / 78 us is at most ONE missed cycle at 10 kHz
   against a 10-cycle trip; even a 64-slot table stays under three. The watchdog will not trip on a
   system that is fine.
2. The deflate fails its own test: zero measured benefit, 2 to 3 us of cost, and it perturbs the
   `thread_effective_prio` donation invariants for that.
3. A priority comparison in `wake()` would cap a woken higher-priority peer's wait at the CURRENT CHUNK:
   13.2 us / 18.9 us at the shipped width, 5.0 us / 8.6 us at width 1. That is a 4 to 9x reduction on
   the number the deflate was meant to fix, and it turns `KCAP_TEARDOWN_CHUNK` into a thread-latency
   knob instead of an ISR-latency one.

## What this capture does NOT establish

- **The mid-sweep switch was not PROVEN safe**, only observed not to break in 1080 deaths. The proof
  obligation is to enumerate every peer that can OBSERVE a dying thread's half-swept table; the soak
  had peers churn their own resources and none reached into the dying thread's.
- **The worst-case denial is derived, not sampled.** Sampled denials (24.8 us XMC, 48.5 us K64F) are
  uniform-phase draws; the worst case is the frozen window, reached when a wake lands exactly at the
  `dying` transition, and that draw was never observed.
- **No table wider than 15 slots was reachable**, so the 64-slot figures are extrapolation.
- **The console-overflow cliff was never entered on silicon**, so its cost is computed.
