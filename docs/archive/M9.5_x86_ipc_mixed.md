# M9.5 x86 mixed IPC decision (2026-09-26)

**Decision: keep one BKL path in the kernel.** The x86 owner-local IPC
experiment was removed after this gate. The [pure-local sweep](M9.5_x86_ipc_local.md)
established a large many-core gain, but it did not measure the cost when global
IPC runs beside it or justify maintaining two exclusion protocols.

This follow-up uses the same 50,000 calls with a checked marker byte per pair
and two payload widths. Every QEMU vCPU was pinned to a distinct physical host
core. Each pair has one endpoint and a server pinned to its numbered core. In
the first `remote_pairs` pairs, the client runs on the next core; the others run
on their server's core. All pairs start together and their active intervals
overlap. The BKL and local-option builds ran the identical workload, in
alternating order, three times each. The [checked TSV](M9.5_x86_ipc_mixed.tsv)
contains each repetition, both payloads, the total rate and separate local
and cross-core rates. Raw QEMU and pin-map captures remain under
`build/m95/mixed{4,12}/`.

| Physical cores | Cross-core pairs | Payload | BKL median/s | Local-option median/s | Change |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 on CPUs 0-3 | 1 of 4 | 8 B | 176,488 | 182,465 | +3.39% |
| 4 on CPUs 0-3 | 1 of 4 | 256 B | 174,979 | 180,436 | +3.12% |
| 12 on CPUs 0-11 | 1 of 12 | 8 B | 155,205 | 319,684 | +105.98% |
| 12 on CPUs 0-11 | 1 of 12 | 256 B | 153,137 | 315,684 | +106.14% |
| 12 on CPUs 0-11 | 6 of 12 | 8 B | 66,458 | 79,868 | +20.18% |
| 12 on CPUs 0-11 | 6 of 12 | 256 B | 65,794 | 79,128 | +20.27% |
| 12 on CPUs 0-11 | 12 of 12 | 8 B | 45,547 | 42,653 | -6.35% |
| 12 on CPUs 0-11 | 12 of 12 | 256 B | 45,355 | 42,475 | -6.35% |

All 24 runs completed every call and passed their marker checks. The 8-byte
candidate and BKL ranges do not overlap in any row: at four cores with one
remote pair they were 180,724-182,497 and 175,498-176,889; at twelve with
one remote pair, 318,417-321,358 and 152,653-156,563; with six remote pairs,
78,976-81,731 and 64,132-67,203; with all twelve remote, 42,396-42,739
and 45,296-46,058. These are aggregate completion rates for a fixed amount
of work, not latency distributions or independent rates per core.

The pure-local 12-core 8-byte result was 9.95x BKL. One cross-core pair cuts
the mixed gain to 2.06x, six cut it to 1.20x, and an all-cross-core workload
loses 6.35%. A failed local probe still enters the local gate before falling
back to the BKL, so cross-core calls pay the writer-barrier protocol without
using a local transaction. On host CPUs 0-3, one cross-core pair left only
a 3.39% gain. The earlier matched single-pair control lost 2.68%, and the
global-only yield control lost 4.42% even without activating the barrier.
The measured benefit does not justify the extra maintained exclusion path.

The 12-vCPU KVM selftest passed ten consecutive runs with the option enabled,
including its parked-local-call migration arm and the available timeout and
thread-death arms. Several order-sensitive reply and slay arms are skipped in
this SMP preset; these runs are a stress sample, not a full concurrent lifetime
proof. Code inspection confirms that cap close and teardown, thread affinity
changes, and mapping changes take the global `IrqLock`. Once local mode is
activated, that lock waits for local spans; a parked local frame releases its
gate only after the outgoing frame is saved, and a migrated frame reacquires
global exclusion. These tests established the experiment's behavior; the
current kernel no longer contains that protocol.

Recheck the retained captures with:

```sh
python3 tools/bench/m95_mixed_report.py build/m95 > docs/archive/M9.5_x86_ipc_mixed.tsv
```

The host is an AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO 370, which [AMD specifies](https://www.amd.com/en/products/processors/laptop/ryzen-pro/ai-300-series/amd-ryzen-ai-9-hx-pro-370.html)
as four Zen 5 and eight Zen 5c cores. Linux reports CPU maximum frequencies
of 5.16 GHz on CPUs 0-3 and 3.29 GHz on CPUs 4-11, consistent with that mapping. Thus
"mixed" means the pinned run used both host core classes; "local" means the
IPC client and server used the same KickOS guest core. Neither term describes
NUMA or cache placement. This KVM result on one host does not change the
two-core LX6 verdict or establish a gain for other CPU mixes.
