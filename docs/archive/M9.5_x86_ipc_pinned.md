# M9.5 x86 IPC with one host core per QEMU vCPU (2026-09-26)

This repeats the [first M9.5 IPC matrix](M9.5_x86_ipc_baseline.md) after
removing a host-placement ambiguity. The first matrix restricted the whole
QEMU process to a physical-core set with `taskset`; Linux could still move
multiple vCPU threads onto one allowed core. `m95_pin_qemu.py` now starts QEMU
with its CPUs paused, finds each KVM vCPU thread, pins vCPU `i` to the `i`th
listed physical host core, records the mapping, then resumes OVMF through QMP.
The process's other threads share the listed CPU set. The one-core KickOS
control still starts two QEMU vCPUs for OVMF; only vCPU 0 runs a KickOS core.
The capture labels `fast` and `slow` name host CPU sets, not a KickOS mode.
This [Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO 370](https://www.amd.com/en/products/processors/laptop/ryzen-pro/ai-300-series/amd-ryzen-ai-9-hx-pro-370.html)
has four Zen 5 and eight Zen 5c cores; Linux reports
5.16 GHz maxima for CPUs 0-3 and 3.29 GHz for CPUs 4-11.

The same eight cases ran three times in alternating order, with 50,000 checked
call/reply transactions per pair at both 8 and 256 bytes. Every pair completed
and its active interval overlapped every other pair's. The parser requires the
recorded vCPU-to-host mapping before accepting a pinned capture. Full per-run
rates, windows, lock-wait and copy data are in
[M9.5_x86_ipc_pinned.tsv](M9.5_x86_ipc_pinned.tsv). Raw captures and pin maps
are under ignored `build/m95/runs-pinned/` in this workspace.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0-1 fast | 1 | 641,157 | 638,352-642,211 | 635,314 | no contended sample |
| 0-1 fast | 2 | 643,656 | 638,275-649,446 | 630,993 | 87 / 636 |

| 4-5 slow | 2 | 413,525 | 413,216-414,165 | 406,016 | 128 / 1,025 |
| 4-7 slow | 4 | 417,045 | 416,948-419,825 | 413,873 | 1,153 / 3,075 |
| 4-11 slow | 8 | 368,889 | 368,065-372,019 | 368,861 | 3,331 / 6,663 |

The four-fast-core aggregate rate remains essentially the one-fast-core rate.
Eight slow cores deliver 89% of the one-slow-core rate. Twelve mixed cores
deliver 45% of the one-fast-core rate, with a much tighter range than the
first sweep. The pinned twelve-core median is 13.8% above the first sweep at
8 bytes and 10.6% above it at 256 bytes; host vCPU placement explains part
of the earlier twelve-core drop, but not the throughput ceiling or the rising
guest lock-wait distribution.

Two additional pinned captures recorded each KVM vCPU thread's host CPU time
over the entire QEMU run, including firmware. In the four-fast-core capture,
QEMU ran for 1.590 s and the four vCPU threads accumulated 1.380, 0.710,
0.640 and 0.640 CPU seconds. In the twelve-core capture, QEMU ran for 5.572 s;
vCPU 0 accumulated 5.190 CPU seconds, and each of vCPUs 1-11 accumulated
4.300-4.500 CPU seconds. Those are separate threads fixed to separate physical
cores, so the IPC test was not a one-host-core run. Whole-run CPU time includes
OVMF and cannot by itself price the IPC portion; the guest's per-pair overlap
and lock-wait rows provide that workload evidence.

Reproduce after building the five `bench_smp_ipc_image` configurations:

```sh
tools/bench/run_m95_matrix.sh 3
python3 tools/bench/m95_matrix_report.py build/m95/runs-pinned > docs/archive/M9.5_x86_ipc_pinned.tsv
```

This remains a KVM guest experiment on one heterogeneous x86 host. It does not
replace the LX6 silicon gate. The later withdrawn owner-local result is recorded
in [M9.5_x86_ipc_local.md](M9.5_x86_ipc_local.md).
