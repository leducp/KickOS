# M8.11 measurements: the descriptor write, the masked span, and what was refused

Silicon captures taken on the three in-tree presets that combine `KICKOS_BENCH` with an enforcing
posture. All three are physical parts: no emulated board in the tree builds an enforcing bench
image, so nothing here can be re-run without the bench.

The `before` column is the milestone's opening commit and the `after` column its tip. Both are
labelled by MILESTONE rather than by hash, which the squash destroys; the banners are in the logs
under `.session/logs/` on the box that took them.

## The ARM descriptor write, measured for the first time

`PH_MPU_COMMIT` had no feed site under `arch/arm` until M8.9 landed one, so every ARM capture this
project has taken read `MPU_COMMIT 0/0 n=0`, including the whole M8.7 rebaseline. These are the
first numbers for it.

| board | posture | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `f411disco` (armv7m PMSAv7, 84 MHz) | enforce | `MPU_COMMIT 247/247 min=247 n=520039` | `189/189 min=189 n=520039` |
| `xmc4800-relax` (armv7m PMSAv7, 144 MHz) | enforce | `244/268 min=240 n=520039` | `220/268 min=197 n=520039` |
| `esp32c6-wroom` (rv32imac PMP, 160 MHz) | enforce | `75/75 min=75 n=520039` | `75/75 min=75 n=520039` |

The `f411disco` and `esp32c6-wroom` after-figures are the milestone's tip. **The XMC pair is NOT**:
its after-figure is the tree that closed the milestone's own items, taken before the review's
findings landed, because the board's cycle counter was dead on both attempts at the tip and the
chain refused each one. Its counter sits in the core debug power domain and comes and goes; what
its pair supports is the SHAPE of the result on a second PMSAv7 part, not a figure at the tip.

The C6 row is unchanged by design: the PMP commit stays total, measured rather than assumed.

**The descriptor write costs more than the switch it follows.** On `f411disco` the switch
distribution is `80/80/232` and the commit 247, so the hardware program is three times the whole
bracketed switch; on `xmc4800-relax` it is `83/83/328` against 244. Both brackets close before the
commit runs, which is why the per-switch ABSOLUTE in every earlier ARM capture understates the
switch by more than the switch.

**The skip is bounded by the instruction stream, not the bus.** The total path is 133 instructions
and 34 PPB stores; the partial path is about 137 instructions and 4 stores. At roughly two cycles a
store that predicts about 60 cycles saved, and `f411disco` moved 58. The two PMSAv7 parts differ in
how much they keep, 25 percent against 10, which tracks their clock and flash wait states rather
than anything in the code.

| board | 8 B round trip before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `f411disco` | 81602 ns | 80434 ns |
| `esp32c6-wroom` | 38325 ns | 38594 ns |

The C6's round trip moved the wrong way by 0.7 percent and its `lock-hold` max by 56 cycles, both
inside the layout sensitivity that board demonstrated at M8.8 with an unexplained 173-cycle step.
Neither is attributed here.

## The masked span, which prices the doorbell copy

The worst-case sweep walks a masked window of a declared width. `KOS_EP_MSG_MAX` is 256, and the
doorbell copies up to two of those per message under the masked body.

| board | bare IRQ entry | span 0 B | 64 B | 256 B | 1024 B |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `esp32c6-wroom` | 192/192/195 | 208 | 832 | 2560 | 10240 |
| `f411disco` | 36/36/47 | 52 | 704 | 2816 | 11264 |

p50 cycles, n=100 a span. So ONE copy at the maximum message size is worth about 15 us of added
interrupt latency on the C6 and 34 us on the F411, against a bare entry of 192 and 36 cycles.

## What these captures do NOT say

- **No MPU-enforcing SMP part exists on this bench**, so the per-core descriptor record is reasoned
  and not witnessed. The record is indexed by the machine's own CPU id because the ARM core MPU is
  banked per core; nothing here runs two cores with an MPU to prove it.
- **The SYSMPU and RX backends carry the skip and have no bench variant**, so neither was measured
  at all. `frdmk64f` and `rx72m` build it and run their suites; no cycle figure exists for either.
- **`xmc4800-relax`'s cycle counter died for one capture** and the chain refused it, a wall of zeros
  over 220000 samples. It read live in the baseline and in the re-take. Its counter sits in the core
  debug power domain and this is its documented character, not a regression.
- **The esp32c6 timer conversion shows nothing here.** Its cost grew with uptime and a bench run is
  seconds old, so the capture cannot see what a long-running machine would.
- **No A53 silicon has ever booted on this bench**, so the unconditional 512-byte vector save per
  armv8a switch is counted from the assembly and its removal is unpriced.
