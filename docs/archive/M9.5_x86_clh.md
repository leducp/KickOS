# M9.5 x86 single-BKL CLH measurement (2026-09-26)

**Decision:** use CLH arbitration for the x86_64 BKL. This keeps one global
critical section and the existing interrupt-mask and doorbell protocol. It
does not split capability, IPC or scheduler ownership. The main
[M9.5 options record](../design-m9.5-bkl-options.md) remains open.

QEMU/KVM ran one vCPU per distinct physical host CPU on a Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO
370, without SMT siblings. Host CPUs 0-3 are the faster class and 4-11 the
slower class. Each active pair made 50,000 synchronous call/receive/reply
transactions for both 8-byte and 256-byte payloads. Values are medians of
three successful ticket and three successful CLH runs of the same image
configuration. The first matrix alternated ticket and CLH. Rates compare
within each row; the 4-fast, 8-slow and 12-mixed rows do not form a controlled
cross-width scaling curve.

The lock-only global yield probe measured 8,644,851 versus 9,346,276
operations/s (+8.1%) on four fast cores, and 2,207,327 versus 3,256,896
(+47.5%) on twelve mixed cores. These are lock costs, not IPC scaling.

The final CLH code leaves the queue-length distribution unsampled instead of
recording a false zero. A fresh comparison of that image against the same
ticket images in the same measurement window gave:

| Placement and pairs | Remote pairs | Payload | Ticket calls/s | Final CLH calls/s | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 fast, 2 local | 0 | 8 B | 624,525 | 639,060 | +2.3% |
| 2 fast, 2 local | 0 | 256 B | 615,600 | 627,264 | +1.9% |
| 4 fast, 4 remote | 4 | 8 B | 70,571 | 72,138 | +2.2% |
| 4 fast, 4 remote | 4 | 256 B | 70,076 | 71,476 | +2.0% |
| 12 mixed, 12 local | 0 | 8 B | 258,891 | 320,453 | +23.8% |
| 12 mixed, 12 local | 0 | 256 B | 250,364 | 317,326 | +26.7% |

The small low-core differences changed sign between batches. Treat them as
near parity, not a reliable 2% improvement. The twelve-core local benefit is
large in both batches. Cross-core IPC remains primarily limited by its
handoff path. This is why CLH is accepted as a contained improvement to one
BKL, not evidence that a BKL split has paid for itself.

The ignored `build/m95/clh-study/` directory has the matched EFI files and
raw captures: `ticket-<tag>-r<1..3>.txt` and `clh-<tag>-r<1..3>.txt` for the
first matrix, plus `final-<tag>-r<1..3>/run.txt` and
`final-ticket-<tag>-r<1..3>/run.txt` for the final comparison. The pinned
runner is `tools/bench/run_m95_ipc.sh`, with host CPU sets `0-1`, `0-3`,
`4-7`, `4-11`, or `0-11` as named in the rows.

One prototype CLH local-pair run timed out at 120 seconds with only OVMF
output. It never printed the kernel entry marker, which precedes lock
execution. Twenty later CLH and twenty ticket boot checks completed, as did
five final twelve-core and one final four-core CLH selftests (`all tests
passed`, clean guest exit). The pre-entry timeout remains unlocalized and is
not evidence of a lock deadlock. The final twelve-core lock benchmark emitted
lock-probe, lock-wait, draw-retry and lock-site reports, then `bench: done`
and a clean exit. `draw-queue` correctly reported no samples. The generic
CTest QEMU bench harness exited before the report on this host, while the
pinned KVM runner completed the same `bench.efi`. The x86 trap-redzone and
static gates passed.
